When you think of being an au pair, you probably think of an agency first. For the US or Australia that's often the normal way. But there's a second way hardly anyone talks about: you find your family yourself. I looked at both and went without. Here's why.

## What an agency does for you

An agency matches you with families, checks them beforehand, helps with paperwork and visas and is your contact if there are problems. For some programs, like in the US, you officially can't do it without an agency. In return you pay a placement fee, and you're tied to the countries, rules and durations the agency offers.

## What's different without an agency

Without an agency you pay no placement fee and choose where you go, how long you stay and which family you really click with. For me that's the biggest freedom: you're not tied to a catalogue. In return you have to sort things out yourself, get to know the family well before you say yes, and think of your own plan B.

## Platforms: the middle way

Between an agency and going fully alone there are platforms like AuPair.com and AuPairWorld. You create a profile, see families from many countries and message them directly. They don't place you like an agency does, but they're a good starting point to find families and have first conversations.

## Destinations with and without agencies

Some countries have lots of agencies, others almost none. French Polynesia is one of those: you'll hardly find agencies placing au pairs there. So if you really want an agency, that limits your options. If you're open to doing it yourself, places open up that hardly anyone else gets to.

## Which one suits you

An agency suits you if you feel safer with someone checking everything for you, and if your dream country requires one anyway. Going without suits you if you like deciding for yourself, are ready to put a bit more work into the search and want to save money.
