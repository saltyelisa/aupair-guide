The question I get the most: "How did you even find a family in Tahiti?" There are hardly any agencies for French Polynesia, and the ones that exist are expensive. The good news is you can do it without one. Here's how I did it and what I'd do differently today.

## Where to find families

Most families don't search on the big platforms but in local Facebook groups and WhatsApp groups. Families post there when they're looking for someone, and you can also write your own post introducing yourself. A profile on the well known au pair platforms is worth it too, because families from French Polynesia show up there now and then.

## Your first message to a family

A good first message is short, personal and in French, or at least includes a few French sentences. Say who you are, why Tahiti, what experience you have with kids and when you're available. A photo where people can see you clearly helps a lot. The guide has ready made templates for your profile and your first message that you only need to tweak.

## Entry and stay

French Polynesia is part of France but is not in the Schengen area and has its own rules for entry and work. What applies to you as an EU citizen depends on how long you stay and whether your au pair stay counts as work.

## Insurance

Your regular health insurance from home usually won't cover you in French Polynesia the way you need. Long stay travel health insurance is a must.

## What does it all cost?

Tahiti is expensive, especially groceries and flights. As an au pair you live and eat with the family, but you should budget for flights, insurance, trips and pocket money for your free time.

## What if it doesn't work out with the family?

It happens more often than you'd think, and it's not your fault. What matters is that you agree on your tasks and hours clearly beforehand, and that you have a plan B.
