Hawaii sounds unaffordable at first. Volunteering makes it doable though: you help out a few hours a day, for example at a hostel, on a farm or at a surf camp, and get accommodation and often food in return.

To be honest upfront: I found both of my spots in Hawaii through personal contacts, not through a platform. If you don't know anyone there, Worldpackers is a really good option though, because it has hosts in Hawaii too. Here's both.

## Way 1: Through contacts

Sounds like luck, but it's often easier than you'd think. Tell people around you and on social media that you want to go to Hawaii. Ask people who've been there if they know someone. And once you're on another Pacific island, new contacts often come up on the spot.

## What to look for in hosts

Whether through contacts or a platform: ask beforehand how many hours per week are expected, where you'll sleep and whether food is included. Read reviews if there are any, especially the bad ones. A good host answers those questions openly.

## Entering the US

As an EU citizen you usually enter for short stays with an ESTA. Whether volunteering in exchange for accommodation is allowed on an ESTA is a question you need to look at closely, because the US is strict about it.

## Costs

Even if you don't pay for accommodation, there's still the flight, ESTA, insurance and everyday life. Food in particular is expensive in Hawaii if your host doesn't include it.
